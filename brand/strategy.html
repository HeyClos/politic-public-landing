01

Assessment

The right subject.
The wrong emotional register.

The existing page makes government information feel consequential. It also asks the reader to believe in speed, breadth, and certainty before showing dependable evidence.

The cinematic chamber sequence is the strongest distinctive asset: it dramatizes the density of public decisions. The navy and warm gold are usable, too. The problem is their surrounding grammar: italic display serif, widely spaced monospace labels, dark panels, ticker, status pulses, and a familiar procession of pain cards, three steps, testimonials, and pricing tiers. In combination, these choices resemble a reusable premium-software template.

This assessment does not establish which model created the page. A font change alone would leave its structure and psychological promise intact.

The deeper trust issue

Live meeting capture and briefing are confirmed. That does not substantiate every city in the ticker, every feature in the pricing tiers, or the stated accuracy rate. Give each claim its own evidence.

02

Audience & emotion

Confidence that can
survive a follow-up question.

A lobbyist puts their own reputation behind the information they pass along. The useful emotional outcome is credible confidence: “I understand this, and I can show you where it comes from.”

NILE’s professional code emphasizes accurate, current information, correction of significant errors, competence, and keeping clients informed. These duties support a brand centered on care and provenance. They do not establish every lobbyist’s priorities. [1]

Clark County’s instructions send readers from meeting details to a File ID and supporting documents; application status requires a separate record, parcel, or address lookup. The opportunity is usable context from public records, without implying privileged access. [2] [3]

One lead audience. A wider mission.

  1. Lobbyists & government affairs teamsPrimary commercial audience. Show the client update, meeting preparation, and source-checking workflow first.
  2. Legislative and elected-office staffAdjacent audience. Emphasize dependable explanations and the ability to relay the record clearly.
  3. Land-use & real-estate teamsAdjacent buyer hypothesis. Demonstrate proposal, stage, jurisdiction, and timing. Parcel watches and matter histories need actual implementation.
  4. Engaged residentsThe mission test. A professional should find it useful without a resident finding it impenetrable.

Positioning

Politic Public makes local government meetings easier to understand and use through clear, source-linked briefs for professional work and public understanding.

Professional clarity is the promise; live capture and meeting briefs are the offer; public access is the mission. Keep each level clear.

Competitors already sell early notice, analyst review, and local monitoring. Those are category expectations. Differentiation must come from a demonstrably better reading experience, specific local knowledge, and honest coverage—not “first,” “only,” or generic intelligence language. [4] [5]

04

Voice & messaging

Write like a capable
colleague has done the reading.

Exact, composed, open, and useful. Use concrete nouns and verbs. Let the evidence carry the authority.

Primary message

Come prepared.

Local government is easier to navigate when the record is clear. Politic Public turns live public meetings into source-linked briefs, built for the next client update, meeting, or policy conversation.

Request early access

Secondary action: Read a sample. Use a real reviewed brief, or explicitly label the illustration.

Message hierarchy

Emotional promise: Come prepared.
Functional promise: Local government, made legible.
Brand signature: Public record. Professional clarity.
Mission: Public information should be useful to the public.

Writing rules

Name the body, meeting, proposal, and source. Explain unfamiliar terms. Attribute claims. State uncertainty directly. Avoid swagger, alarm, vague superlatives, and language suggesting the software replaces professional judgment.

Existing framingThe gavel satisfies no one who wasn’t listening.

New framingKnow what happened. Bring the context.

Existing framingThree minutes. Every material motion.

New framingA clearer account of what happened—with the source close at hand.

Existing framingZero filler. No interpretation.

New framingThe discussion, in plain language. The record, one click away.

Existing framingThe pulse before the press conference.

New framingPublic record. Professional clarity.

Summarization necessarily involves editorial choices. Explain the method instead of claiming the absence of interpretation. “Request early access” also matches a beta more honestly than suggesting immediate delivery or an assured response time.

05

Visual identity

Public in spirit.
Precise in execution.

Keep the familiar ink-and-gold relationship, but change its proportions. Paper becomes the working surface; ochre marks attention.

The Courier brings the public record within reach. An attentive bird carries a document toward the reader. Its three raised tail feathers, three curved wing cuts, and wiry, uneven feet preserve the personality of the chosen drawing. The stance conveys readiness; the document gives that energy a useful purpose. The irregular contours are intentional. They make the mark approachable beside the precise, source-led language of the product.

The signature pairs the Courier with the stacked lowercase Barlow Condensed 700 wordmark. Use the complete signature for first encounters. Keep the full bird at least 48px high; use mark-small.svg below that size, including the favicon. Preserve proportions and clear space of at least one quarter of the bird’s height. Use ink on light surfaces, paper on ink, and ochre as a restrained accent. Keep the bird facing right, retain its uneven silhouette, and avoid effects, extra outlines, or seal-like enclosures.

Aim for roughly 70% light surfaces, 25% ink, and 5% accent. Set dark text on ochre; reserve pale rules for decoration rather than essential controls. Never use color alone to communicate status. Provide visible focus and sufficient contrast for each actual pairing.

Barlow Condensed · 700 · Headlines

A CLEARER VIEW
OF LOCAL GOVERNMENT.

Public Sans · 400 / 500 / 600 / 700 · Reading & interface

Understand the proposal. Follow the source. Bring useful context to your next conversation.

Typography

Use condensed display type for short headlines, never paragraphs. Body: 16–18px, 1.6 line height, about 60–70 characters per line. Prefer sentence case for interface text. Keep metadata readable.

Composition

Build around a reading column, strong left alignment, purposeful asymmetry, and horizontal rules. Use whitespace to separate ideas. Avoid a repeating grid of rounded feature cards, floating pills, decorative gradients, and excessive shadows.

Graphic language

Use document edges, annotations, source markers, and measured divisions. Diagrams should explain actual information. Avoid fabricated dashboards, meaningless charts, stock handshakes, and capitol imagery implying official affiliation.

Identity in use

Keep the same hierarchy in email briefs, sample records, presentations, and social excerpts. The source and meeting context should remain visible when content is shared independently of the landing page.

06

Motion & page architecture

A meeting becomes
a useful record.

Follow one item from the agenda to a readable brief. Give the visitor time to understand it, then let the complete record rest on the page.

  1. Agenda
    A housing proposal and its parking requirement.
  2. Amendment
    A proposed change to the parking condition.
  3. Vote
    7 in favor, 2 opposed, 1 abstained.
  4. Brief
    Amendment passed. Final project approval pending.

A coherent fictional example, visibly labeled as illustrative. It demonstrates the distinction between an amendment and a final approval; it is not current government activity or a product-interface recording.

Motion rules

The first three scenes each last 4.2 seconds, with 400ms opacity crossfades. Text and fixed vote totals are visible from the first frame; secondary detail gains emphasis over one second. After 12.6 seconds, the complete brief remains until Replay. Use paper and ink, muted green, and a small ochre underline. Keep the Courier static. Exclude camera shake, cut flashes, number counters, and continuous loops.

Reading sequence

Promise and current scope → readable sample → practical professional uses → source and method → exact coverage → early-access invitation. Replace generic social proof with inspectable product proof until real customer evidence exists.

Reader control

Provide Pause, Play, and Replay according to playback state. Suspend animation offscreen and in hidden tabs. Reduced-motion preferences show the complete final brief immediately with playback disabled. Keep ordinary interface transitions to 160ms.

The approved application

The main landing page uses this treatment. The motion comparison and original montage archive document the design decision. The headline and next action remain readable throughout.

Motion should leave the reader more composed and better oriented. The finished brief matters more than the transition into it.

07

Promise discipline

Make precision
part of the brand.

A narrower promise that survives inspection is a better foundation for professional trust than an impressive capability list.

  • Lead with the operating product.The founder confirmed on 17 October 2026 that live meeting capture and briefs are operating. Present this confidently. Older agenda-only specifications are stale technical documentation, not evidence that live capabilities are unavailable.
  • Define what live means.Live capture does not automatically establish instant delivery, complete coverage, or an accuracy rate. State supported bodies, source types, publication timing, and handling of missing or unclear records. Separate the recording stage from brief availability.
  • Keep adjacent capabilities distinct.Parcel watches, matter histories, archives, exports, instant alerts, and geographic breadth need separate confirmation. Live capture and briefing do not imply these features. Describe discussions, votes, and amendments with the distinctions actually present in the record.
  • Distinguish evidence from explanation.Make sources, editorial interpretation, unknowns, and corrections legible. Document how AI and human review are used in the operating process. “Reviewed” is a process claim that must be earned for each published brief.
  • Keep commercial proof real.The founder requested retaining the existing AWS, nClouds, StartupNV, and SBA backing logos on 20 October 2026. Other testimonials, accuracy rates, turnaround figures, and pricing entitlements remain omitted. Do not replace them with invented evidence. Price professional workflows after testing their usefulness and willingness to pay.

For real estate, the official association analysis workflow asks for proposed text, timing, and likely industry impact. That supports concrete contextual information rather than a generic industry feature tile. It does not establish demand for this particular product. [6]

08

Validation & rollout

Test the feeling
against the work.

Validate what professionals understand and trust before treating visual preference as product-market evidence.

Interview 5–8 lobbyists.

Include independent practitioners, firm staff, and in-house government affairs; mix land-use and other domains. Ask each to reconstruct a recent meeting-to-client-update workflow using redacted materials. Identify verification steps, missed context, current tools, and who pays.

Observe a real task.

Give participants a real meeting record and reviewed sample. Ask them to identify the action, date, source, and unresolved question, then prepare a client update. Compare comprehension and source checking; ask what they would refuse to forward.

StageActionWhat to learn
1 · Message testShow the page briefly; ask for an unaided explanation of product, coverage, and availability.Whether “Come prepared” connects to an understood, concrete offer.
2 · Trust testTest source retrieval and proposal-versus-decision comprehension. Ask which claims require evidence.Whether the design supports accurate confidence rather than misplaced certainty.
3 · PilotRun a small, clearly scoped brief pilot. Collect corrections and observe repeat use.Whether the output helps an actual professional workflow and what remains missing.
4 · RolloutConfirm claims and form behavior; check accessibility, mobile layout, motion controls, and analytics.Whether qualified visitors can evaluate and request the offer reliably.
Measure quality of intent.

Track sample engagement, source-link use, qualified early-access requests by role, repeat brief use, and correction rate. Pair these with comprehension interviews. Establish a baseline before setting targets; page conversion alone cannot prove trust, usefulness, or willingness to pay.

These interviews can expose problems, not establish market percentages. Investigate matter tracking: a selective brief may fall short of professional needs for completeness, continuity, and reliable updates.
